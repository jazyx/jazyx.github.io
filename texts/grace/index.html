Grace

The darkness fills her eyes. Perhaps, she thinks, this is what Death is like. She closes her eyes and opens them again. There is no difference, no difference at all. Perhaps all she has to do is close her eyes and wait for something to happen. Then she remembers why she came here, and she opens her eyes, wide open.

She has to keep moving forward. Somewhere there will be light.

"Grace!"

It's not like Grace to come down this late, not on a school day. Her mother picks up the pile of folded clothes that is on the second step, and carries it up the stairs. She needs both hands to hold the pile steady, so she uses her elbow to knock on Grace's door.

No answer.

Awkwardly, she presses on the door handle, and pushes the door open. It is still dark inside. The curtains are still closed.

"Grace?"

Her mother puts the clothes in an empty space on top of the dresser. She opens the curtains. In her bed, Grace does not move. Her mother brushes the back of her fingers against Grace's cheek.

No reaction.

Anxiety mounting now. Shaking Grace by the shoulder. Checking for her breathing. Running downstairs to grab her mobile phone. Turning panic into action.

The hospital waiting room. Lucy's mother's hand on her shoulder.

"Mary? What are you doing here?"

"Jennifer! How is Lucy today?"

Jennifer sits down heavily on the hard hospital chair beside her. "They are preparing to give her the bone marrow transplant. I suppose that's a good thing. I mean, it's something. They wouldn't do it if it had no chance of helping, would they?"

A pale smile. Then an expression of concern. She studies Mary's face.

"So what's wrong? Why are you waiting here for?"

"Grace didn't wake up this morning. They've taken her to do tests."

"Didn't wake up? I can imagine that you are worried." Mary puts her hand on Jennifer's. "You have been worrying about little Lucy and her leukaemia now for so long, it seems selfish for me to feel worried about my Grace."

A tunnel of darkness. The ground is soft and uneven. The walls are strangely warm. Grace moves forward, her arms outstretched, pushing against the narrow walls. There are sounds all around, regular rhythms. Steady streams and wind that sighs and lifts and dies. And something else, something random, in the distance. The thing that she has been dreading.

Voices in her head. Not real voices. Voices created by the blackness, filling the emptiness of her eyes.

"Go. Go back. You are mistaken. You are lost. Losing yourself. This path leads nowhere..."

So deep. A sense of being buried. There is only one way forward, and it is getting tighter. She must keep moving. She must keep her eyes wide open.

"Back, back!" the voices tell her. "This is not your world. You are not welcome here."

The voices press her from all sides. All kinds of voices, and unkind voices. Hissing and booming, whispering and snarling. Inhuman voices, the voices of friends, voices of indifference. "This is no place for you. Go back. Go home."

Grace needs her hands to find her way forwards, she cannot block her ears. And anyway, that would not help. The voices are inside her head. And none of them are Lucy's.

"Mrs Shaw?" The nurse looks business-like. Mary sees no sign of tension in her.

"Yes, I'm Grace's mother. Can I see her now? Is she going to be OK?"

"Doctor Walker has done a preliminary examination, and he has found no signs that cause him to worry about your daughter. She is resting peacefully. We have taken a blood sample and we have sent it to the laboratory for analysis. We must wait for the results. We will keep your daughter here, under observation, and we will call you as soon as there is something new to tell you."

"Resting peacefully? No signs? Have you ever seen this sort of thing before? Can I see her now?"

"You can relax now, Mrs Shaw. Grace is in good hands. Every person who comes in here is different. Almost everyone goes home very soon after they come in. And yes, of course, you can see your daughter. You can sit quietly with her and see how calm she is. Her tranquility can reassure you."

Mary wants to do something, something dramatic. She wants to fight for her daughter. The nurse's voice envelops her in silence, wraps an invisible blanket around her so that it is difficult to move. She follows the nurse along the white corridor, listening to her footsteps, listening to the hospital smells, listening to the wordless churning inside herself. Grace's face is so smooth, so vulnerable, so still. Mary sits beside her and takes hold of her hand.

"Will it hurt, Mummy? Will it hurt more?"

Jennifer doesn't want to lie. "I don't know, Lucy," she says. "The doctors have ways to keep the pain away." "They give me medicine and needles. I want to be outside." "I want you to be outside. Climbing trees. Splashing in puddles. Roundabouts, slides. Kites in the park. When the operation is over, and you are feeling well again, we will spend lots and lots of time outside, in the sun and the rain and the wind."

Lucy smiles and stretches her arms upwards, so that her mother will hug her. Her arms are so thin, and so hard to lift. She likes to feel her mother's hair against her cheek. She has no strength to pull her close, she lets her arms go limp on her mother's back and whispers in her ear, "Grace is here."

Jennifer feels her muscles tighten. She makes herself close her eyes and breathe in the smell of Lucy's skin. She doesn't want to lie. She never wants to lie to her. But not all truths are good.

"Yes, she's here. She's like a big sister for you, isn't she?"

"She's coming to see me?" There is excitement in Lucy's small voice.

"Maybe later. You have something important to do, very soon. The nurse will be here soon."

"Hug me, Mummy. Hug me until she comes."

"Stop! Do not cross the line!"

Someone somewhere there in the darkness. Grace can see nothing, no-one, but she can feel a presence.

"Papers!" The word is thrown at her, all sharp edges.

"Papers?" she asks. "What about papers?"

"Passport, identity card, driver's licence, proof of domicile. Papers!"

"I have no papers. I'm just me. Please let me go on."

She feels someone coming towards her. Someone small and very solid. Someone as hard as cannonballs. A hand reaching out towards her, uncaringly. Reaching into her, through her, tearing something from her. Pulling it out.

And then there is light. All at once she can see, everything.

The stone-faced goblin pulling something glowing out of her, its face white and shadowless in the light, the line that she must not cross, and beyond an immense space. Not a tunnel any more: a hugeness that no light can fill.

"What's this, then?" the goblin asks, holding the glowing thing up menacingly in front of her eyes. "Look at the state of it! Tattered and torn and stuck together. I can't accept this."

"That's ... mine," says Grace. "I made it. It's my soul."

"Ha!" the goblin snorts. "Your soul, you say. Hogwash and poppycock! A child's scrapbook, more likely. Look at this! Disgraceful..."

Grace watches as the goblin opens it like a book, and turns the pages. The goblin's fingers create great black shadows across the world.

"Grace..." the goblin reads, running one of its black finger across a page.

"Yes," says Grace.

The goblin turns more pages. There are loose sheets of paper. Some of them are torn. Some fall to the darkness of the ground, but the goblin ignores them. Grace picks them up carefully, and smooths them in her hands so that they shine again.

"That's not yours. It shouldn't be in here. What are you doing with pages of other people's souls, eh, child?" The goblin gives her no time to answer. "I will have to confiscate them. Take them from you. This will have to be reported. You will come with me."

But Grace does not want to go where the goblin is going. She has no time for this. As soon as the goblin turns its back, she is across the forbidden line and running, running, running through the empty immensity with the few shining papers in her hand.

Lucy is in a strange room, with no windows, full of metal things. She holds her mother's hand and watches the nurse. The nurse talks to her gently, she runs her hands softly down the side of Lucy's neck. Lucy is not listening to the words. She is listening to silent squeezing of her mother's hand.

The smell of expected pain on a swab of cotton wool, and then its sharp softness against her skin. She prepares herself for the needle. So many needles. Always needles.

The nurse's face is motionless. And then she smiles again. Her mother touches her face, moves her hair away from her eyes. Her mother's fingers are warm.

She cannot see what the nurse is doing now. She cannot feel the nurse's fingers any more. She watches her mother's face. Her mother is not looking at the nurse. The nurse is doing something to her neck that her mother does not want to see.

Lucy has no strength to do anything but lie still and let it happen. Her mother is with her, that is all that matters.

"The bone marrow is flowing into her bloodstream now through the catheter. It will take about 6 hours. Over the next few weeks, the new bone marrow cells will find their way into Lucy's bones, and they will start to create new blood cells, good blood cells, without any of the leukaemia blasts."

The nurse holds her hands apart, palms towards each other, bouncing slightly as if measuring the weight of something. She turns her right hand palm upwards. The good news.

"The new bone marrow cells can do two different things: they can find the leukaemia cells that Lucy's own bone marrow has created, and they can destroy them. Graft versus tumour, we call it. And that's good."

Left hand, palm down.

"But they can also turn against the whole body. Graft versus host. We have drugs that can reduce the chances of this happening, and the drugs have their own side effects. We must wait now, and see. It can take a long time."

"A long time is good," says Jennifer. "A long time together is everything we can hope for."

"Mary?"

It is night time now. The hospital is quiet. Jennifer is familiar with its rhythms. She stands in the doorway to the room where Grace is still not moving. She has two coffees on a tray, and some sweet pastries.

"How is she? Any change?"

Mary turns her head slowly to look at her. Shakes her head.

"And Lucy?"

"Sleeping."

"It would be so good if they would put them in the same room, side by side, so that our girls could wake up together," says Mary.

"They will never do that. They want to keep their patients separate, so that they only see what they want them to see."

"Ah! There was something. I saw it on the monitor. Grace's heart started beating faster, and then after a few minutes it slowed down again."

"That's good, isn't it?" says Jennifer. She holds the cardboard cup of coffee out for Mary to take.

"It's something, I suppose."

The immensity is narrowing. The walls on either side are hard and brittle and the colour of blood. There is a warm sweet unpleasant smell, and the air is getting thicker. Grace imagines decaying and burning leaves, or something metal, rusting. Something neither alive nor dead.

With each step forward, it becomes harder to hide the truth from herself. Up ahead, it's not a dragon. It's something worse.

"Grace?" Lucy's voice is tiny. She opens her eyes. She squeezes the hand that is holding hers.

"It's me, Lucy," her mother says. "Grace is ... sleeping now."

"She was with me. In my dreams." Lucy is smiling. "She's waiting with me. She put a coat around my shoulders. She said it was her strength. Is she cold now?"

Jennifer squeezes Lucy's hand. "She's warm. She's resting." "She had food. But she gave it all to me."

"She's not hungry now, Lucy. You can rest."

She had imagined a dragon. Something huge and hideous and angry and strong. But not this. Not this. Not this emptiness. Despair turning in on itself. A hunger devouring itself. Huge, yes and hideous.

If only it were dead, or undead. But Grace can feel the life within it, grasping, grasping. Gasping and failing and unable to die.

Hopelessness giving birth.

Over and over and over.

Filling Lucy's blood with useless deathless blasts.

This is not something that Grace can fight. She cannot even approach it. It sucks the the hope out of the air around her. The scraps of paper in her hand glimmer, a guttering flame. She stands still. She will not move away. She has come this far through darkness. She has come this far with a purpose and she will not fail. She will find a way.

A memory.

Lucy a new-born baby. Lucy entrusted to her arms, the sensation of her hour-old skin against her own skin. Her fragility. And the strength of life glowing within her.

Grace fills herself with this memory. She lets it blossom within her, until it is the only thing she knows. She sinks to the ground, her arms a cradle of emptiness, and she becomes one with this memory, with this moment.

There is nothing else but this beautiful moment and that waiting hunger.

And then she lets it go. She lets the hunger feed on her memory, and she lets a dark sliver of emptiness enter into her in its place.

Her name is Grace. Grace is the strength that is born of weakness. Grace is the thanks you give before you eat. She can be stronger than this hunger. She can give herself up to this hunger, piece by piece by peace and still be stronger. That is what she has come here for. Her name is Grace.

A hand on her shoulder. Jennifer's hand. Mary turns her head and presses it against Jennifer's wrist, but she cannot raise her eyes, cannot even open them.

"Still sleeping?" says Jennifer's voice, from miles and miles and miles away. "It will be good when you can see certain signs of improvement."

"Yes," Mary hears herself say. Then she holds on tight to the word that does not change. "Sleeping."

She makes a tiny movement of her head towards the monitor. She cannot do more. She cannot frame her thoughts into sounds. She wants Jennifer to look, but she does not want her to see. She does not want her to see that the signals are growing weaker.

Lucy on a bicycle, the training wheels taken off, and only Grace to give her balance. Running behind her, holding her small body steady, steadying her each time she almost falls and then the fear of falling inverts itself into laughing. Danger past. Running behind her holding just the seat, so that Lucy cannot feel that she is still supporting her. And then just running behind her, holding on to nothing, so that Lucy does not know that she is on her own.

Then running past her, in front of her, turning to see the joy of understanding on Lucy's face, and catching Lucy in her arms to stop her headlong motion, falling on her back onto the ground with Lucy in her arms, gasping for words through her laughter.

Another memory to relive, to bring back to life. Another memory to hold onto for a while, another memory to run with, for a while, and then to let go.

Grace. Race. One piece at a time.

"Mary, you need to sleep now. I'll take you home. If Grace wakes while you are sleeping the nurses will let you know." Mary feels exhaustion walking with her. The car door opening, hands to guide her into the seat. The world slipping by outside. Jennifer's voice, and then a familiar softness to lie on.

"I'll just take off your shoes, and put a blanket over you. I'll take a nap too, on the couch next door."

Not sleep. Just thoughts swirling in darkness. Then nothing. Beautiful nothing.

Lucy's first day of school, and so proud in her new red school pullover. So proud to stand in the same school yard as her big friend Grace. Lucy breathing in such deep contented sighs. Raising her hand when her name is called, and then her smile as she climbs the stairs and goes into the school. Her school now.

And then for Grace, the smell of the freshly cut grass. The gentle wind tugging at her skirt. The seagulls mewing and clattering onto the roof. The weight of her schoolbag in her hand. The voices of her friends, their faces changed after a summer apart. All these sensations, and Lucy the centre of them all.

She was standing then. She stands up now. She forces herself to move closer to the hunger. The light she holds is so dim now.

Grace is the final merciful shot that takes away a life that can be lived no longer. But she is not that grace. Grace is what you give when you give and want for nothing in return.

"Mummy?"

"Yes, Lucy?"

"I want to see Grace now. Will they let me walk?"

"Grace is sleeping."

"No. Your mouth says she is sleeping, but that is not what your eyes say." Lucy's face is serious. White on a white pillow. "I want to see Grace now. Please."

"Yes. You are looking stronger now. I'll call the nurse. I'll ask her to come with us."

The air is as thick as wind but it is not moving. Grace forces herself forward through the dimness. Forward is the only way to go. Into the rising darkness. All that is left of her. And then she is touching something. Something warm.

Something that moves against her skin. Something that presses itself against her. A face against her cheek.

The hunger was not like this. Not warm. Not alive, not like this. This is something else.

And suddenly something bursts inside her. Something so big that she cannot hold it in.

Laughter.

The perfect sound of paradox. The sound that belongs to consciousness. The sound of being separate and being one. One thing and its opposite in the same breath. Without a soul to see the world, a soul that can see itself, a soul that can find absurdity and ridicule and impossibilities, there can be no laughter. The sound of life itself.

Lucy is laughing and Grace is laughing and Mary and Jennifer and the nurses are laughing, and the hunger has a soul again and it is giving birth to life.

"How are you feeling, Lucy?" Grace says as she hugs Lucy to her. "How are you feeling now that you are whole again?"
